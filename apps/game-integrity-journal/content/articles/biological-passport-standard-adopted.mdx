The International Federation of Endurance Sports has formally adopted a revised Athlete Biological Passport standard that lowers the statistical threshold at which unusual blood-value fluctuations trigger a targeted investigation, the federation confirmed Tuesday.

## What's changing

The passport system tracks an athlete's blood markers over time rather than testing for specific substances directly, flagging patterns consistent with blood doping even when no prohibited substance is detected. Under the new standard, the probability threshold used to flag a profile drops from 99.99% to 99.9% specificity — a change that anti-doping scientists say will roughly double the number of profiles referred for expert review each year.

<Callout label="Why specificity matters">
  A tighter specificity threshold catches more genuine anomalies but also raises the rate of false positives requiring follow-up. Federations have historically erred toward higher thresholds to avoid contested cases — a trade-off critics say has let real cases slip through.
</Callout>

## Independent reaction

Dr. Elena Vasquez, a hematologist who has consulted for anti-doping panels but was not involved in the rule change, called it "a meaningful, if overdue, correction," noting that the previous threshold had not been revisited since 2016 despite substantial improvements in profiling software.

Athlete representative groups offered cautious support, with one coalition asking IFES to publish clear appeal timelines for athletes whose profiles are flagged under the new standard.

The change takes effect at the start of next season and will apply retroactively to samples already on file.
