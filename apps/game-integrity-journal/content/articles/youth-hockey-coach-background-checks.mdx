The Northfield Youth Hockey Association's board voted unanimously last week to overhaul how member clubs screen and track coaches, following an internal review that found sanctioned coaches had, in at least four cases over the past three seasons, resurfaced at a different member club without disclosure.

## What the old system missed

Under the previous policy, background checks were conducted once, at initial certification, and results were held by individual clubs rather than shared association-wide. A coach removed from one club for a conduct violation was not flagged in any centralized system a neighboring club could check before hiring.

<Callout label="What's changing">
  Starting next season, all NYHA-affiliated coaches will be re-screened annually, and any confirmed conduct violation will be logged in a shared registry visible to every member club at the point of hiring — not just the club where the violation occurred.
</Callout>

"We were relying on word of mouth to catch problems the system should have been catching automatically," said an NYHA board member who worked on the policy review. "That's not good enough for an organization responsible for kids."

## Independent oversight

The new registry will be administered by a third-party background-check vendor rather than NYHA staff directly, a change advocates for youth-sports safety reform have pushed for across multiple sports as a way to reduce conflicts of interest in how misconduct findings are handled internally.

The policy takes effect at the start of the next registration cycle. NYHA said it will publish an anonymized annual summary of registry activity, beginning with the following season.
